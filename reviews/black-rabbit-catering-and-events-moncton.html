Black Rabbit Catering and Events Review

329, Moncton, NB

★★☆☆☆ 2/5 Not Recommended

Went to Black Rabbit in Moncton, NB in September of 2023 for a birthday celebration, because we had heard good things about it from the Internet.

We appreciate quality cuisine, but it seems that true fine dining hasn't quite made its way to Moncton, New Brunswick yet. This review will center on the excessive use of foam and peculiar creativity we encountered during our “fine”dining experience at Black Rabbit.

The good:
The dining room's cozy modernity promised an intimate culinary journey. The service sparkled with genuine smiles and helpfulness, and the bread, simple yet satisfying, was a comforting anchor in a sea of gastronomic uncertainty.

The not-so-good: The kitchen, resembling a teen drama cast more than a professional culinary team, led us to question, "Where's the chef?"

Dining alongside only one other party at 7 PM felt eerily quiet for a supposedly popular spot.

The oysters were very fresh and after dousing with lemon, the accoutrements for it including the horseradish were very bland.

The watermelon sashimi was extraordinarily silly. Simply dusting a slice of watermelon with Mrs. Dash seasoning and placing it atop compressed rice doesn't magically transform it into a convincing sashimi substitute for meat eaters.

The appy of seared scallops, tasty by themselves, were sadly lost in a confusing muddle of blueberries and an unnecessary yellow foam. Why ruin perfectly good scallops with such frivolity?

Our main courses continued this trend of foam fascination. The tenderloin sat forlornly on a scallop like whatever, and was simply boring. A little roux on the side would have been nice. A little seasoning of the meat or charring would have been nice. That was a very lonely dish, its only companions being aimless swirls of foam.

The squid ink pasta was not very adventurous. I don’t even remember what kind of shredded meat was thrown on it, although I think it was supposed to be rabbit. Does rabbit come in a can? Because that’s what it tasted like. Again accompanied by a side of foam decoration.

Cocktails were the only standard fare, a safe harbor in that stormy sea of culinary experiments.

In the end, Black Rabbit, has got the service down pat, but someone controlling the kitchen is overzealous and misguided in their creativity and it leaves much to be desired. If not for the welcoming service, I'd seriously wonder how they’ve managed to hop along in Moncton's food scene for so long.

Reviewed by MsJAG, Level 7 Google Local Guide. Self-funded visit, no sponsorship.
